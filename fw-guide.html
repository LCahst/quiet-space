Getting started in faction warfare

Faction warfare is fought over complexes: combat sites you capture by staying inside them, which push the system toward your side and pay you loyalty points. This guide walks through it in the order you’ll meet it: what enlisting means for you, finding and reading complexes, which ships fit where, and what you earn.

Before you enlist: staying alive and supplied

Joining a militia puts you at war everywhere, not just in the warzone. Enemy militia pilots can attack you anywhere in New Eden, high-sec included, without CONCORD stepping in. Pirate militias are at war with two empires at once, so they feel this twice as much.

Fighting is the easy part to plan for. What catches new pilots out is everything around it: getting ships, modules and ammo to where the fighting is, and getting home again.

Why logistics get harder

  • The whole route is dangerous. Most of what you buy comes from a high-sec trade hub, and enemy militia can catch you on any jump between the hub and your base, not only once you reach low-sec. High-sec is where many new pilots feel safest, and it’s often where they get caught.
  • Some hubs are off limits. If you’re at war with the empire that owns a trade hub, its navy will attack you there. Pick a hub you can reach safely before you enlist.
  • Ships can get stranded. In enemy-held systems behind the frontline (their Command Operations and Rearguard systems), you can only dock or undock in a capsule, not in a ship. Anything you left there stays there until you move it another way.

Why alts help

  • A dedicated faction warfare alt keeps your main free to use high-sec and every trade hub as normal. This is one of the most popular set-ups, and it matters even more for pirate militias.
  • If you enlist your main, a support alt that stays out of the militia can buy at the trade hub, haul to your staging system and contract items to you, with no war targets on it. It can also take contracts for ships stranded in enemy-held systems.
  • Keep the alt in a different corp. When a corporation enlists, every character in it is enlisted too, alts included.

Why a faction warfare corp helps

Whether you fly a main or an alt, joining a faction warfare corp is the single best thing you can do to stay in the fight. It helps in almost every part of faction warfare:

  • Supplies: most corps keep fitted ships on corp contracts in their staging system, so you can replace a loss without a trip to the trade hub.
  • Ship replacement (SRP): many corps pay back some or all of the ships you lose on corp fleets.
  • Intel: shared intel channels and Discord tell you where the enemy is before you undock.
  • Fleets, leadership and training: experienced pilots who will show you fits, tactics and when to run.
  • People to fly with. Friends make losses easier to shrug off and wins more fun, and a group survives far better than a pilot alone.

Many corps stage from a high-sec system on the edge of the warzone, which the enemy militia can’t enter without their navy coming after them. Ask about staging and logistics when you look for a corp; good ones will have answers ready.

The standing you need

To enlist in an empire militia, your character needs an unmodified standing of at least 0.0 with that empire: the Amarr Empire, Caldari State, Gallente Federation or Minmatar Republic. You can check it on the Standings tab of your character sheet.

“Unmodified” matters. Skills like Diplomacy and Connections raise your effective standing, which helps with things like agent access, but they don’t count here: only your real, base standing does.

  • Old missions may have cost you. Running missions for one empire usually lowers your standing with its rivals, so a pilot with years of Caldari missions behind them can find their Gallente standing below zero. Check before you settle on a side.
  • Fighting in faction warfare won’t cost you standing with other empires. Since April 2026, capturing complexes, killing the enemy’s NPCs and running faction warfare missions cost nothing with any empire. What still costs standing is declining or failing missions, and shooting your own side (see Friendly fire and awoxing).
  • Joining as a corporation: the corporation’s standing, the average of its members’ standings, must be 0.0 or more. Since June 2026 every member must also be above -5.0 when the corporation applies; anyone below that is listed, and the corporation can remove them or apply later.
  • Joining a corporation that’s already enlisted also needs you to be above -5.0 with its empire.

If you’re below 0.0, see Fixing your standings below.

Fixing your standings

If your standing with the empire you want is below 0.0, here are your options, roughly from most useful to most limited.

Run the Sisters of EVE epic arc Best for most pilots

The Blood-Stained Stars is a 51-mission level 1 chain for the Sisters of EVE. Near the end you choose which empire to help, and that empire gives you a 7% standing gain (8.75% with Social V). None of the other empires lose any standing for it.

  • No standing needed to start: talk to Sister Alitura in Arnon, Essence 0.8.
  • It all happens in high-sec, and a Tech 1 destroyer is enough. It’s mostly travel and can be done in a few hours.
  • You can run it again every 90 days. A standing gain is a share of the distance left to +10, so one run lifts you from about -0.7 to 0.0, or from about -0.9 with Social V.

Join a faction warfare corporation Easiest

A corporation enlists on its members’ average standing, so a corporation of pilots in good standing can carry you if you’re only a little below zero, as long as you’re above -5.0. You also get experienced corpmates to fly with, which counts for a lot in faction warfare.

Train the right skills Passive

  • Social raises your real standing gains from missions by 5% per level, so it helps you reach the 0.0 you need. Train it before running the arc or missions here.
  • Diplomacy (for factions you’re negative with) and Connections (for factions you’re positive with) raise effective standing only: useful for reaching higher-level agents, but they won’t get you past the enlistment check.

Turn in pirate tags at a data center One-off

Each empire has a data center where agents give faction standing in return for pirate tags, which you can loot or buy on the market. The level 1 agents want 3 copper or 20 bronze tags; higher levels want 20 of pricier tags and need 1.0, 3.0 or 5.0 standing, so below zero you’re limited to level 1.

  • The gains are small at level 1: about 1.7% each for the Gallente agents, the only ones with published figures.
  • Failing a data center mission costs standing, so only accept one when you have the tags ready.

Run the career agents One-off

If you haven’t already, each empire’s career agents give standing for easy tutorial missions: three sets per empire, each worth about 7.5%. They only count once per character, and you can find them in the Agency window.

Run empire COSMOS missions One chance only

COSMOS missions are storyline chains whose final missions give faction standing. Each empire has a constellation of them: Araz in Kador (Amarr), Okkelen in The Forge (Caldari), Algintal in Sinq Laison near Dodixie (Gallente) and Ani in Metropolis, reached only from Hek (Minmatar). COSMOS agents only look at faction standing: level 1 agents need none, but level 2 needs 2.0, so below zero you’re limited to the level 1 chains.

You can only ever do each COSMOS mission once. Failing one, or letting it expire, locks you out of that mission and the rest of its chain forever. And as soon as you talk to the agent, you get an offer that expires in seven days, so don’t speak to them until you’re ready to run it.

Boost your gains with a Glamourex booster Event or store

Glamourex boosters work like extra levels of the Social skill: the Potent version gives +50% to standing gains. Take one before the Sisters arc or a COSMOS chain to get much more out of it. There are two ways to get them, and they behave differently:

  • From in-game events such as Capsuleer Day: these are items that stop working after a set date, so check the description before you buy one on the market.
  • In packs from the official EVE Online store: these don’t go stale. They wait in your unclaimed items until you redeem them, but they’re applied to your character the moment you redeem them and only last a limited time from then, so redeem one just before you start the arc or missions.

Use a standings service Costs ISK

Some player corporations raise NPC standings for you, for a fee paid in ISK, the in-game currency. One long-running service advertises on the official EVE forums and has a Discord server. These are run by players, not CCP, so read how they work and check their reputation before you send any ISK.

Once you’re in, faction warfare keeps your standing up. Military Campaign objectives give standing with your empire, with no losses elsewhere, and each promotion in your militia gives a one-off standing bonus.

How to enlist

  1. Open Theaters of War: in the Neocom (the menu at the top left), go to Activities → Theaters of War.
  2. Choose your side: pick Empire faction warfare, then the empire you want to fight for. (The Pirate option is covered in our pirate guide.)
  3. Enlist while docked at a station owned by that empire. Khanid Kingdom and Ammatar Mandate stations don’t count as Amarr.
  • If you’re in an NPC corporation, enlisting moves you into your empire’s militia corporation: the 24th Imperial Crusade (Amarr), State Protectorate (Caldari), Federal Defense Union (Gallente) or Tribal Liberation Force (Minmatar).
  • Joining as a corporation: a corporation’s CEO or a director can enlist the whole corporation, and it takes effect after the next downtime. Every member joins along with it, alts included.
  • Leaving: you can retire from the militia at any time while docked, but you’ll have to wait 24 hours before you can enlist again.

Friendly fire and awoxing

“Awoxing” means attacking pilots on your own side. In faction warfare, everyone in your militia counts as your side, even pilots in other corporations, and the game punishes friendly fire through your standing with your empire.

  • Every attack on a militia mate costs you standing. Since March 2026 the penalty is doubled inside faction warfare sites, battlefields included, and halved outside them.
  • Fall below -5.0 and you’re out. You get a warning at downtime, and if your standing isn’t fixed 24 hours later, you’re removed from your corporation. If you’re the CEO, the whole corporation (or alliance) is removed from faction warfare instead.
  • Low standing also keeps you out: below -5.0 you can’t join an enlisted corporation, and a corporation can’t enlist while any member is below it.
  • Accidents count too. Area weapons like smartbombs hit everyone in range, friend or foe, so be careful where you use them.
  • To recover, Military Campaigns and the options in Fixing your standings all raise your standing with your empire.

Reading complex names on the probe scanner

Complexes show up as combat sites in your probe scanner, and their names tell you everything you need before you warp. Take this one:

Gallentewhose side the complex belongs to
Smallthe size, which sets the biggest hull allowed
ADVthe tier, which sets which kinds of ship are allowed
-1how many pilots the reward scales to

That last number matters for your wallet. The loyalty point (LP) reward for capturing a site is shared among the pilots in it, and it scales up to that many pilots. Bring more than the number shown and each pilot’s LP payout starts to shrink. A “-1” site is built for one pilot; a “-4” site can reward four without anyone losing out.

The first time anyone warps to a complex, it appears on everyone’s overview in that system, so you can warp straight to it after that. You can also spot ships sitting in an active complex with a narrow, short-range directional scan (combat recon ships are the exception: they don’t show up this way).

The four tiers, from strictest to most open

Basic (BSC)
Tech 1 ships only.
Navy (NVY)

Advanced (ADV)
Tech 1, navy, pirate and Tech 2 ships.
Elite (ELT)
Everything, Tech 3 included.

Open complexes sit outside this system entirely: there’s no gate at all, so any ship can fly in, from a frigate all the way up to a titan.

The exceptions that catch people out

  • Tech 3 ships need a bigger gate than their size suggests. A Tech 3 destroyer can’t use a Small advanced complex, but fits into a Medium advanced one. A Tech 3 cruiser can’t use a Medium advanced complex, but fits into a Large advanced one. The one special case is an elite site built for Tech 3 destroyers, the Contested Field Research Facility.
  • Some ships count as pirate ships, even if they don’t look it. Sisters of EVE ships (Astero, Stratios, Nestor), Society of Conscious Thought ships (Metamorphosis, Sunesis, Gnosis, Praxis), EDENCOM ships (Skybreaker, Stormbringer, Thunderchild) and Triglavian ships (Damavik, Vedmak, Drekavac, Leshak) are all treated as pirate ships, so they need an advanced or elite gate.
  • Assault frigates have their own site. Assault frigate complexes let only assault frigates in, which makes them a good place for an even fight.
  • Some sites reward the right hull. Elite sites give Tech 3 destroyers an extra 500 shield, armor and hull, and Moderate sites boost battlecruiser skill bonuses by 20% (Command Ship skills excepted), so those are the places to bring them.
  • Moderate advanced sites only appear during pirate insurgencies. Normal faction warfare has the Moderate navy site; the advanced version spawns in insurgency systems from stage 2.

Rewards: victory points and loyalty points

Every site you capture does two things. It moves the system toward changing hands, measured in victory points (VP): it takes 75,000 VP to make an enemy system vulnerable, and capturing sites in your own system takes VP away from the attacker. And it pays you loyalty points (LP), the militia currency you spend in its LP store.

To get either, you have to stay inside the capture area until the site’s timer runs out: about 5 minutes for the smallest scout sites, 10 for other frigate and small sites, and 15 for everything bigger. The table gives each site’s timer. The NPCs guarding a complex now come in at most five waves instead of respawning forever, so once they’re cleared, only other players can stop you.

How reliable are these numbers? Faction warfare has changed many times, and no single source has everything. We cross-checked CCP’s own patch notes, the EVE University Wiki and eve-fw.net. How the LP works

  • Where you plex matters most. Frontline systems pay 50% more than command operations systems, and rearguard systems pay almost nothing (a 99% cut).
  • LP is split, not earned per minute. Everyone inside the capture radius when the timer finishes gets an equal share, however long they were there. Leave early and you get nothing.
  • Defending pays only when it’s needed. Capturing a site in your own faction’s system pays the full reward while that system is contested or vulnerable, and nothing once it’s stable.
  • Three sites only spawn in frontline systems: Scout BSC-1, Field Research ELT-5 and Moderate NVY-3, so they always pay the frontline rate. Scout BSC-1 is a solo Tech 1 frigate site whose NPCs spawn twice as often and are more likely to carry warp disruptors; Field Research applies three random effects (from a pool of 20) to everyone inside.
  • Battlefields are the big ones: 150,000 LP for the winning side. How battlefields work.

Supply caches and advantage

Each side has an advantage in every frontline and command operations system, and the side ahead gets more victory points for each complex it captures there. What counts is the gap between the two sides’ totals. Battlefields give the biggest boost; smaller sites and structures nudge it up or down.

Supply caches

  • What they are: NPC structures belonging to one faction, also called supply depots. Each faction’s caches spawn in frontline and command operations systems, in its own space and the enemy’s, several times a day.
  • Destroying one takes 2% advantage away from its faction in that system.
  • It pays 15,000 LP to every pilot who damaged it, however long ago they shot it, so you don’t need to be there for the final blow.
  • They’re guarded by a small NPC fleet of frigates, destroyers and cruisers, and the structure has a fair amount of hit points. Clear the guards first or bring enough active tank, mainly against EM and thermal damage. Since Cradle of War (June 2026), caches have the same all-round resistances as the NPCs in complexes.
  • You can repair your own side’s caches. Since 11 November 2026, remote repair works on supply caches and depots, so you can keep one alive while the enemy is shooting it.

Faction warfare missions

Besides capturing complexes, you can run missions for your militia. They pay LP and ISK, but they don’t give victory points or change who holds a system, so they’re a way to earn, not a way to win the war.

  • Who gives them: agents of your militia corporation (the 24th Imperial Crusade, State Protectorate, Federal Defense Union or Tribal Liberation Force), in low-sec stations in the warzone. You have to be enlisted, and agents in systems the enemy holds can’t be used.
  • Four levels: level 1 needs no standing; levels 2, 3 and 4 need 1.0, 3.0 and 5.0 with the agent or their militia corporation.
  • Every mission is combat: you kill enemy navy ships or destroy a structure, usually in the enemy’s half of the warzone. The NPCs are about one level easier than in ordinary missions.
  • Gates limit your ship. Since 2026 mission sites use navy gates, small for level 1, medium for level 2 and large for level 3, so only Tech 1 and navy ships of the right size get in, just like complexes. We couldn’t find a published gate size for level 4.
  • No standing losses for the fighting. Since April 2026, killing the enemy’s NPCs and structures in these missions costs no standing with any empire. Declining or failing a mission still costs standing with the agent’s corporation; you can decline once every 4 hours per agent without a penalty.
  • Rewards: LP and ISK when you finish, plus navy dog tags from the NPCs, which you can sell or spend in the LP store. Since November 2026, haulers in these missions drop tags too, and bosses like the Field Commander drop more. The NPCs pay no bounties. The Minmatar militia’s ISK guide, using October 2026 prices, puts a level 4 mission at about 30,000 LP and 3 million ISK, or 35 to 70 million ISK an hour in a cruiser; that’s their estimate, not an official figure.

Staying alive on missions

  • Mission sites aren’t hidden. Once you enter one, it shows up as an anomaly that anyone in the system can warp to without scanning, enemy militia included. Keep an eye on d-scan and local.
  • Get in, do the objective, get out. Kill only what the mission needs, and pick up several missions before you head out so one trip covers them all.
  • Older guides may be out of date. Advice from before the 2026 gate changes, like running level 4s in stealth bombers, may no longer work, so check what the gate allows before you fit a ship.

Faction warfare missions changed a lot in 2026 and current guides are thin, so treat this as a starting point. If something here doesn’t match what you see in game, trust the game.

Battlefields

Battlefields are the biggest sites in faction warfare: large objectives built for organised fleets of 25 pilots or more, with the biggest rewards to match. They work differently from complexes, so here’s how.

Where and when they spawn

  • Only in frontline systems, and no more than two exist at any one time.
  • When one is won, a new one appears a few hours later. The EVE University Wiki gives three hours; CCP’s September 2026 notes describe battlefields as respawning every four.

Getting in

  • Tech 1 and navy ships only. Pirate, Tech 2 and Tech 3 ships can’t use the gate (CCP’s own name for the site, “Battlefield NVY-30”, is a navy-tier site for 30 pilots).
  • The acceleration gate sits farther out than usual, 10 to 20 km from the beacon rather than right beside it.
  • There are three landing spots inside: one for each militia and one for neutral pilots. Pilots who aren’t in a militia land 75 to 125 km from the capture points, so they arrive late to the fight.
  • Cloaking isn’t allowed inside a battlefield.
  • Rally Point beacons have their own overview group, “FW Rally Point”, so add it to your overview to see them.

Capturing a battlefield

  • There are three capture points: one at the top-middle of the site and two about 80 km out to either side, so a fleet has to spread out or move between them.
  • Holding a point adds progress for your side each time it ticks, and the first side to reach 100% wins the battlefield.
  • Enemy NPCs, or enemy militia pilots, within 30 km of a point pause its progress, so you have to clear the area and hold it, not just sit on the beacon.
  • Only pilots in ships count: a pod can’t capture or hold a point.
  • Expect a long fight: pilot guides put a typical battlefield at around 30 to 45 minutes.

What winning gives

  • 2,000 victory points toward taking (or defending) the system.
  • A very large boost to the winning side’s advantage in that system, which makes that side’s other captures there worth more victory points.
  • 150,000 LP for the winning side, shared fully by up to 30 pilots; with more than 30, each pilot’s share shrinks.
  • Attacking can pay better than defending: CCP has described a defender LP penalty, which makes contesting a battlefield in your own frontlines worth less than one in the enemy’s.

Battlefield rules have changed several times since they arrived in 2022, most recently with Cradle of War in 2026, which briefly cut the LP to 50,000 and added NPC freighters before both changes were reversed two weeks later. If something here doesn’t match what you see in game, trust the game.

Spending your LP

You spend LP in your militia’s loyalty point store, where most offers also cost some ISK and a few need other items too. You can fly what you buy, or sell it for ISK, and how much ISK each LP turns into varies hugely from offer to offer. Since November 2026 each store also sells its empire’s navy battlecruiser (Oracle Navy Issue, Naga Navy Issue, Talos Navy Issue or Tornado Fleet Issue, 52,000 LP and 13 million ISK for the blueprint copy) and its empire’s Synchronizer rigs. Two free tools compare every offer for you:

  • Fuzzwork LP Store: pick a corporation to see each offer’s ISK per LP, priced as a 5% buy at Jita, with Jita trading volume beside it. It can also search for which stores sell a given item.
  • Eden’s Loyalty, the LP comparator by Arin Mara: one sortable table of every LP store offer in the game, with the cost of any required items, the 5% buy price, the 5% buy volume and the ISK per LP, priced in The Forge.

Before you buy something to resell:

  • Go by the 5% buy price, not the sell price. Sell orders show what other sellers hope to get. The 5% buy price, the average of the highest 5% of buy orders, is much closer to what you’ll actually get if you sell straight away, and one stray order can’t distort it.
  • Check the volume. A great ISK per LP means little if hardly anyone buys the item: it might take days or weeks to sell, and if other pilots are cashing in the same offer, the market can be flooded and the price drops as you sell. It’s common for the top offers by ISK per LP to show little or no buy volume, so an offer with a lower ISK per LP that trades in large numbers is often the better deal.
  • Prices move. These tools are a guide, so check the market in game before you spend a large amount of LP.
  • Ask your corporation about an LP buyback. Many faction warfare corporations buy their members’ LP for ISK at a set rate, a simple, steady way to cash in without hauling or trading.

Pirate insurgencies

Want to fly for the pirates instead? Our pirate faction warfare guide covers joining the Guristas or the Angel Cartel, how insurgencies work, and fixing your standings to enlist.

  • While pirates are running an insurgency in a system, every complex there is an advanced one, so more kinds of ship can get in.
  • If a system reaches the highest corruption level (stage 5), low-sec starts to behave much like null-sec, warp bubbles included. Fly with that in mind.
  • You can follow live insurgencies, corruption and suppression on our faction warfare map with the pirate focus button.
