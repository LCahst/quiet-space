Pirate faction warfare

Faction warfare isn’t only for the four empires. Two pirate factions, the Guristas and the Angel Cartel, run their own militias and launch insurgencies: week-long campaigns to corrupt empire systems while the empires fight to suppress them. This guide covers how to join, what joining means for you, how insurgencies work, and how to fix the standings that stop many pilots from enlisting.

The standing you need

To enlist, your character needs an unmodified standing of at least 0.0 with the pirate faction you want to join.

This changed in September 2025. When pirate faction warfare launched with the Havoc expansion in 2023, the minimum was -2.0. CCP raised it to 0.0 in version 23.01, so older guides that say -2.0 are out of date.

“Unmodified” matters. Skills like Diplomacy and Criminal Connections raise your effective standing, which helps with things like agent access, but they don’t count here: only your real, base standing does. If you’re below 0.0, see Fixing your standings below.

How to enlist

  1. Open the Insurgencies window: in the Neocom (the menu at the top left), go to Activities → Insurgencies.
  2. Choose your faction: click Open Faction Selection, switch to the Pirates tab, and pick the Guristas Pirates or the Angel Cartel.
  3. Get somewhere you can enlist: if you aren’t docked at one, click Initiate Enlistment, which sets a route in your Neocom. You can enlist in two kinds of place:
    • The Fulcrum in Zarzakh, for either faction. Zarzakh’s gates connect to low-sec systems (Alsavoinon in Placid and Turnur in Metropolis), so it’s usually the safer trip, though Turnur has risks of its own. Until you’ve enlisted you’ll pay Zarzakh’s gate toll, and you can only leave through the gate you came in by for six hours.
    • Any station owned by your chosen faction, but these are in null-sec (Venal for the Guristas, Curse or Fountain for the Angels), where anyone can attack you and warp bubbles are allowed. Travel in something cheap and fast.
  4. Enlist: dock at the station, open the window again and click Enlist Me.
  • Leaving: you can retire from the militia at any time while docked, but you’ll have to wait 24 hours before you can enlist again.
  • Joining as a corporation: a corporation’s CEO or a director can enlist the whole corporation, and every member joins along with it.

What enlisting means for you

Enlisting makes you a legal target for two whole militias. Read this before you click Enlist Me.
  • You’re at war with two empires. Guristas are at war with the Caldari and Gallente; Angels with the Amarr and Minmatar.
  • Their militias can shoot you anywhere in New Eden, high-sec included, without CONCORD stepping in and without any hit to their security status. (The same goes for you shooting them.)
  • Their faction navies will hunt you in their high-sec space. If you jump into a high-sec system owned by an empire you’re at war with, its navy ships will come after you and hit hard, so don’t linger. Systems caught up in an active insurgency are the exception: from corruption stage 2, navy and police ships stop spawning there.
  • At high corruption, the rules of engagement change. In a system at the highest corruption stage, high-sec loses most of CONCORD’s protection and low-sec allows warp bubbles and bombs. See Corruption and suppression.
  • Shopping gets harder. The biggest danger is other players, not NPCs: as a pirate you’re fair game for two enemy militias instead of one, and their pilots know where the trade hubs are. Unless you use an alt, you’ll have to haul every ship and module you buy yourself, and the systems around trade hubs become some of the most dangerous places you can fly. Two of the four big hubs are also in high-sec your enemies’ navies patrol: Jita and Dodixie for the Guristas, Amarr and Rens for the Angels.
  • Have a plan before you enlist. A trading and hauling alt who stays out of the militia, or a faction warfare corp that keeps ships on contract, turns supply runs from a gamble into a chore. Our faction warfare guide covers this in more detail.

How insurgencies work

An insurgency is a pirate campaign against one warzone. It’s a tug-of-war: pirates corrupt systems by doing pirate activities, and the empires suppress them by doing anti-pirate activities. Whichever side pushes enough systems all the way wins, and everyone who helped on that side gets paid.

The life of an insurgency

  1. A Forward Operating Base (FOB) appears. This pirate structure is placed in a low-sec faction warfare system near the frontline, and it’s the pirates’ base for the campaign: pirate militia can dock, fit and tether there, and the Zarzakh Shipcaster sends them straight to it.
  2. Forecasting, for 48 hours. The FOB’s location and the first four target systems are revealed, so both sides can prepare, but no sites spawn yet and the FOB can’t be attacked.
  3. The active phase, up to 7 days in all. Insurgency sites start spawning, and both sides race to push systems to the top stage.
  4. It spreads. When a system reaches corruption stage 3, a neighbouring system (security 0.0 to 0.8) joins the insurgency and starts spawning sites too.
  5. It ends in a pirate win, an empire win or a tie, and the payouts go out.

CCP shortened insurgencies from 14 days to 7, and doubled the forecasting period from 24 hours to 48, in September 2026.

How each side wins

  • Pirates win by pushing their target number of systems to corruption stage 5. They then take temporary control of the FOB’s system. When the insurgency ends, they hand it back to its old owner, but its infrastructure hub becomes vulnerable about 24 hours (and one downtime) later, giving either empire militia a quick chance to flip it.
  • Empires win by pushing their target number of systems to suppression stage 5. That makes the FOB vulnerable, and they then have to destroy it, after a single armor reinforcement timer of 3 hours (give or take an hour). Destroying it raises suppression by one stage in every insurgency system, and the insurgency winds down over 48 hours.
  • Neither side reaching its target ends in a tie, with no bonus payout for anyone.

The ambition modifier

The ambition modifier stops one side from winning insurgency after insurgency. Each side’s target starts at 5 systems, and winning makes your next target harder.

  • Winning adds +1 ambition to the winning side, so it needs one more system at stage 5 next time. A side on a streak can climb to +5, meaning 10 systems.
  • Only one side can have ambition at a time. If the underdog wins, it doesn’t gain ambition straight away: instead, the favourite’s ambition drops by 1. Only once the favourite is back to the neutral 5 systems do further wins start building the other side’s ambition.
  • Losing with ambition costs 1, down to the minimum of 5 systems.
  • Where to see it: each side’s current target is shown in the Insurgency window, and the in-game pirate insurgency guide in the Agency explains it too.

Activities and how they pay

Every completed activity adds to the system’s corruption (for pirates) or suppression (for the empires) and, for most of them, pays LP to the pilots who did it. On top of that, if your side wins the insurgency, every pilot who contributed gets 1,500,000 LP plus an ISK payout at the end.

Insurgency complexes (Corporate Outpost Raids)

These work like faction warfare’s advanced complexes: clear the defending NPCs, then hold the capture point until the timer runs out. Pirates fight Mordu’s Legion guards; empire pilots fight the pirate NPCs. They can spawn at any corruption level, and allow Tech 1, navy, pirate and Tech 2 ships of the right size.

Other insurgency activities

  • Mining Op Ambush (from corruption stage 1, 1–5 pilots, any ship, built with cruisers in mind): an ORE Rorqual has been tackled by pirates. Pirates hold off the Mordu’s Legion mercenaries sent to save it until it pays its ransom, which earns LP and corruption; destroying the Rorqual instead lets you loot it and still adds corruption, but pays no LP. Empire pilots kill the pirate ransomers until the Rorqual can warp away, for LP and suppression. The LP is shared fully by up to 5 pilots.
  • Ice Refinery Heist (corruption stages 3 and 4, 5–30 pilots): the big fleet activity. Both sides escort automated fuel tankers from an ORE refinery back to their own scoring zone while trying to steal the other side’s; the first to deliver 10 tankers wins 150,000 LP (base). It respawns every 4 hours. NPCs deal full damage and keep coming in waves, and pirates who fly too close to the Upwell Thunderchild guards get destroyed.
  • Reward crates from named NPCs: once a system’s corruption or suppression is high enough, roaming NPCs start warping around it, including rare named ones (Zohar’s Chosen for the Angels, Hakuzosu’s Steelscale for the Guristas, and Upwell ‘Kingbird’ fortifications for the empires). Their loot can be turned in for reward crates: pirates at the Pirate Laundering Facilities in the FOB system, empire pilots at the Counter-Insurgency Outposts in their faction’s War HQ system (Onnamon for the Caldari, Intaki for the Gallente, Mehatoor for the Amarr, Amo for the Minmatar).
  • EVE Vanguard: insurgency missions in CCP’s shooter, EVE Vanguard, also feed into corruption and suppression.

Corruption and suppression

Every insurgency system has a corruption level (the pirates’ side) and a suppression level (the empires’ side), from 1 to 5. Each completed activity adds a percentage toward the next level (see the table above), and every level brings new effects for the side that earned it.

  • Seeing the levels: the Insurgency window shows each system’s levels, a message pops up when you jump into an affected system, and systems at the top corruption stage show a ▼ LAWLESS marker in your route.
  • The two sides push against each other: from suppression stage 3, corruption builds 30% slower in that system, and a fully suppressed system can’t be corrupted past stage 4.
  • Stage 5 is what wins insurgencies, and it also brings the biggest changes, especially the loss of CONCORD protection in highly corrupted high-sec.

Zarzakh, the pirate hub

The story so far. Zarzakh is a hidden Jovian system at almost the exact centre of New Eden, deep in the void of Divinity’s Edge (Yasna Zakh, as the Amarr call it). Dust and gas swirl through it on their way into a black hole known as the Point of No Return, or Duzna Kah to the Amarr, so visibility is poor and much of the system is still unexplored.

In 2023, during the Operation Epiphany event, capsuleers tracked down four ancient Jovian stargates that led there, and the gates opened that September at Fanfest. Someone had beaten them to it: the Deathless Circle, a coalition of criminals and renegades drawn mostly from Caldari and Minmatar organised crime, with Thukker smugglers in the mix, led by a mysterious figure called the Deathless. They had learned to work the Jovian gates and made the Jovian megastructure the Fulcrum their base. The Deathless then offered the Guristas and the Angel Cartel a sanctuary hidden from the empires, and when the Havoc expansion brought insurgencies in November 2023, Zarzakh became the home of pirate faction warfare.

Getting in and out

  • Four ancient gates lead in, one from each direction: Alsavoinon (0.3, Placid) to the west, Turnur (0.4, Metropolis) to the east, H-PA29 (-0.2, Venal) to the north and G-0Q86 (0.0, Curse) to the south. The two low-sec gates are the safer way in.
  • Non-pirates pay a toll on the way in: the gate asks you to confirm a fee that rises with your ship’s mass, starting around 10,000 ISK for small ships. Pirate militia pilots don’t pay.
  • The Emanation Lock: once you enter through a gate, you can only leave through that same gate for 6 hours. Coming back in through a different gate resets the timer and locks you to that one instead. The Shipcaster, clone jumping and losing your pod all get you out regardless.
  • Capital ships can’t gate in, freighters included. The Orca is the one exception.

Turnur, the eastern gateway

Turnur (0.4, Metropolis) is one of the best-connected low-sec systems in New Eden: it has a gate straight into Zarzakh, sits where several regions meet, and wormholes regularly open there to all sorts of places, which makes it a handy shortcut to many destinations. That same traffic and geography make it dangerous, with plenty of pilots passing through and hunting there, so if you travel through it, know the risks and don’t fly anything you can’t afford to lose.

EVE-Scout keeps a free, up-to-date list of Turnur’s current wormhole connections (and Thera’s), so check it before you set off.

The Fulcrum

  • Who can dock: anyone who isn’t enlisted with an empire militia, as long as they don’t have a weapons or retribution timer.
  • Services for everyone who can dock: repairs, fitting, market, insurance and industry.
  • For joining: you can enlist here with either pirate faction.
  • For pirate militia: jump clones, the Guristas and Angel Cartel LP stores, offices for pirate-enlisted corporations, and a 90% lower SCC surcharge.
  • For builders: a 6% material and 70% time bonus when manufacturing sub-capital Guristas and Angel Cartel ships.

The Deathless Shipcaster

  • Pirate militia pilots can launch straight to their faction’s Forward Operating Base in the current insurgency, skipping the trip across New Eden.
  • Each launch has to charge first, and there’s a cooldown between uses, so check its status before you plan around it.

Hazards and dangers

  • Stay near the safe zones. The gates, the Fulcrum and the Shipcaster sit in protected zones. Leave them and the environment starts eating your hull, faster the longer you stay out.
  • Bookmarks and directional scan don’t work in Zarzakh, so you can’t make safe spots or scout ahead the usual way.
  • Gate guns fire missiles at anyone with a retribution timer, and they also paint and jam their targets.
  • Deathless NPC fleets (“Zarzakh” and “Outer Zarzakh”) patrol the gates and stations, here and in the neighbouring systems. They attack anyone not aligned with the pirates, and anyone with a retribution timer.
  • Empire militia pilots often camp the system despite all that, so it isn’t the safe haven it was meant to be. Watch local before you undock.

Fixing your standings

If your standing with the pirate faction is below 0.0 (common for anyone who has run missions against pirates), here are your options, roughly from easiest to hardest.

Join a pirate faction warfare corporation Easiest

When a corporation enlists, every member is enlisted with it, so your personal standing stops being the obstacle. You also get experienced corpmates to fly with, which counts for a lot in faction warfare.

Train the right skills Passive

  • Social raises your real standing gains from missions by 5% per level, so it helps you reach the 0.0 you need. Train it before running the arcs or missions below.
  • Connections raises your effective standing with empire factions you’re already positive with. That helps you reach the 3.0 needed for the empire agents that start the pirate epic arcs.
  • Diplomacy (for factions you’re negative with) and Criminal Connections (for pirate factions you’re positive with) also raise effective standing only: useful for agent access, but they won’t get you past the enlistment check.

Run your pirate faction’s epic arc Best payoff

Each pirate faction has an epic arc, a mission chain whose final mission gives a large boost to your base standing with that pirate faction (shown in the arc as +30%). You can repeat an epic arc every 90 days.

The useful trick: you can start them from empire agents, so even if your pirate standing is poor, you can begin with good empire standing instead. You need at least 3.0 effective standing with the agent, their corporation or their faction, and no worse than -2.0 with anyone involved.

  • The arcs themselves run in pirate null-sec (Venal for the Guristas, Curse for the Angels), where gate camps and warp bubbles are common. A fast ship that can’t be bubbled, like an interceptor, makes life much easier.
  • They cost some empire standing: Smash and Grab lowers your Caldari standing (by 2.4% to 12%) and your Gallente standing slightly; Angel Sound lowers your Minmatar standing by about 2.4%.

Run pirate COSMOS missions One chance only

COSMOS missions are short storyline chains that give faction and corporation standing. The Guristas’ are in the E-8CSQ constellation in Vale of the Silent, and the Angels’ are in the I-3ODK constellation in Feythabolis, both in null-sec.

You can only ever do each COSMOS mission once. Failing one, or letting it expire, locks you out of that mission and the rest of its chain forever. And as soon as you talk to the agent, you get an offer that expires in seven days, so don’t speak to them until you’re ready to run it.

Boost your gains with a Glamourex booster Event or store

Glamourex boosters work like extra levels of the Social skill: the Potent version gives +50% to standing gains. Take one before an epic arc or COSMOS chain to get much more out of it. There are two ways to get them, and they behave differently:

  • From in-game events such as Capsuleer Day: these are items that stop working after a set date, so check the description before you buy one on the market.
  • In packs from the official EVE Online store: these don’t go stale. They wait in your unclaimed items until you redeem them, but they’re applied to your character the moment you redeem them and only last a limited time from then, so redeem one just before you start the arc or missions.

Use a standings service Costs ISK

Some player corporations raise NPC standings for you, for a fee paid in ISK, the in-game currency. One long-running service advertises on the official EVE forums and has a Discord server. These are run by players, not CCP, so read how they work and check their reputation before you send any ISK.
