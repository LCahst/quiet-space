Pirate faction warfare

Faction warfare isn’t only for the four empires. Two pirate factions, the Guristas and the Angel Cartel, run their own militias and launch insurgencies: week-long campaigns to corrupt empire systems while the empires fight to suppress them. This guide covers how to join, what joining means for you, how insurgencies work, and how to fix the standings that stop many pilots from enlisting.

The standing you need

To enlist, your character needs an unmodified standing of at least 0.0 with the pirate faction you want to join.

This changed in September 2025. When pirate faction warfare launched with the Havoc expansion in 2023, the minimum was -2.0. CCP raised it to 0.0 in version 23.01, so older guides that say -2.0 are out of date.

“Unmodified” matters. Skills like Diplomacy and Criminal Connections raise your effective standing, which helps with things like agent access, but they don’t count here: only your real, base standing does. If you’re below 0.0, see Fixing your standings below.

How to enlist

  1. Open the Insurgencies window: in the Neocom (the menu at the top left), go to Activities → Insurgencies.
  2. Choose your faction: click Open Faction Selection, switch to the Pirates tab, and pick the Guristas Pirates or the Angel Cartel.
  3. Get somewhere you can enlist: if you aren’t docked at one, click Initiate Enlistment, which sets a route in your Neocom. You can enlist in two kinds of place:
    • The Fulcrum in Zarzakh, for either faction. Zarzakh’s gates connect to low-sec systems (Alsavoinon in Placid and Turnur in Metropolis), so it’s usually the safer trip. Until you’ve enlisted you’ll pay Zarzakh’s gate toll, and you can only leave through the gate you came in by for six hours.
    • Any station owned by your chosen faction, but these are in null-sec (Venal for the Guristas, Curse or Fountain for the Angels), where anyone can attack you and warp bubbles are allowed. Travel in something cheap and fast.
  4. Enlist: dock at the station, open the window again and click Enlist Me.
  • Leaving: you can retire from the militia at any time while docked, but you’ll have to wait 24 hours before you can enlist again.
  • Joining as a corporation: a corporation’s CEO or a director can enlist the whole corporation, and every member joins along with it.

What enlisting means for you

Enlisting makes you a legal target for two whole militias. Read this before you click Enlist Me.
  • You’re at war with two empires. Guristas are at war with the Caldari and Gallente; Angels with the Amarr and Minmatar.
  • Their militias can shoot you anywhere in New Eden, high-sec included, without CONCORD stepping in and without any hit to their security status. (The same goes for you shooting them.)
  • Their faction navies will hunt you in their high-sec space. If you jump into a high-sec system owned by an empire you’re at war with, its navy ships will come after you and hit hard, so don’t linger. Systems caught up in an active insurgency are the exception: from corruption stage 2, navy and police ships stop spawning there.
  • At high corruption, the rules of engagement change. In a system at the highest corruption stage, high-sec loses most of CONCORD’s protection and low-sec allows warp bubbles and bombs. See Corruption and suppression.

How insurgencies work

An insurgency is a pirate campaign against one warzone. It’s a tug-of-war: pirates corrupt systems by doing pirate activities, and the empires suppress them by doing anti-pirate activities.

How one runs

  • It starts from a Forward Operating Base (FOB), a pirate structure placed in a low-sec faction warfare system near the frontline. Pirate militia can dock, fit and tether there.
  • Forecasting (about 48 hours): the FOB’s location and the first four systems are revealed, but no sites spawn yet and the FOB can’t be attacked.
  • Active phase: insurgency sites start spawning. The whole insurgency lasts up to 7 days.
  • It spreads: when a system reaches corruption stage 3, the insurgency spreads to a neighbouring system.

How each side wins

  • Pirates win by pushing enough systems to corruption stage 5. They then hold the FOB’s system for a while, and after the insurgency it becomes easier for either empire to flip.
  • Empires win by pushing enough systems to suppression stage 5, which makes the FOB vulnerable, and then destroying the FOB. Its armor reinforcement timer is 3 hours, give or take an hour.
  • How many systems is “enough” starts at 5 and is adjusted by the ambition modifier: each win raises the winning side’s target by one (up to 10 systems), so a side on a winning streak has to work harder.
  • Neither side reaching its goal ends in a tie, with no bonus payout.
  • The winning side’s contributors share big rewards, including 1,500,000 LP each.

What you’ll actually be doing

  • Capturing insurgency complexes: Small, Medium, Moderate and Large sites that work like faction warfare complexes, with 10 to 15 minute capture timers and ship-size and pilot limits. They add 0.25% (Small) to 1.5% (Moderate) corruption or suppression each.
  • Corporate Outpost Raids (from stage 0), Mining Op Ambushes (from stage 1), where an ORE Rorqual is under attack, and Ice Refinery Heists (stages 3 and 4), where you escort fuel tankers; ten tankers wins it.
  • Turning in loot from named NPCs for reward crates: at the FOB’s system for pirates, or at your faction’s headquarters for the empires.
  • Missions in EVE Vanguard also feed into corruption and suppression.

Corruption and suppression

Every insurgency system has a corruption level (the pirates’ side) and a suppression level (the empires’ side), from 1 to 5. When you jump into an affected system, a message tells you what’s in effect, and systems at the top corruption stage show a ▼ LAWLESS marker in your route.

Zarzakh, the pirate hub

Zarzakh is a special system near the centre of New Eden, built as a base for pirate-aligned pilots. Its station, the Fulcrum, is run by the Deathless Circle.

Getting in and out

  • Four gates lead in: from Alsavoinon (Placid), Turnur (Metropolis), H-PA29 (Venal) and G-0Q86 (Curse).
  • Non-pirates pay a toll on the way in, based on ship mass. Pirate militia pilots don’t pay.
  • The Emanation Lock: once you enter through a gate, you can only leave through that same gate for 6 hours. You can always leave by the Shipcaster, by clone jumping, or by losing your pod.
  • Capital ships can’t gate in (the Orca is the exception).

What’s there for pirates

  • You can enlist here with either pirate faction, without going into pirate null-sec.
  • The Deathless Shipcaster sends pirate militia pilots straight to their faction’s FOB in the current insurgency.
  • Pirate LP stores for the Guristas and the Angel Cartel, and jump clones for pirate militia pilots.
  • Market, fitting, repairs and industry, with bonuses for building Guristas and Angel ships and a 90% lower SCC surcharge for pirate militia.

Watch out for

  • Bookmarks and directional scan don’t work in Zarzakh.
  • Gravity damages your hull once you leave the safe areas around the gates, the Fulcrum and the Shipcaster.
  • Gate guns and roaming NPC fleets attack anyone with a retribution timer, and the NPC fleets also attack non-pirates.
  • Empire militia pilots often camp the system, so it’s not as safe as it was meant to be.

Fixing your standings

If your standing with the pirate faction is below 0.0 (common for anyone who has run missions against pirates), here are your options, roughly from easiest to hardest.

Join a pirate faction warfare corporation Easiest

When a corporation enlists, every member is enlisted with it, so your personal standing stops being the obstacle. You also get experienced corpmates to fly with, which counts for a lot in faction warfare.

Train the right skills Passive

  • Social raises your real standing gains from missions by 5% per level, so it helps you reach the 0.0 you need. Train it before running the arcs or missions below.
  • Connections raises your effective standing with empire factions you’re already positive with. That helps you reach the 3.0 needed for the empire agents that start the pirate epic arcs.
  • Diplomacy (for factions you’re negative with) and Criminal Connections (for pirate factions you’re positive with) also raise effective standing only: useful for agent access, but they won’t get you past the enlistment check.

Run your pirate faction’s epic arc Best payoff

Each pirate faction has an epic arc, a mission chain whose final mission gives a large boost to your base standing with that pirate faction (shown in the arc as +30%). You can repeat an epic arc every 90 days.

The useful trick: you can start them from empire agents, so even if your pirate standing is poor, you can begin with good empire standing instead. You need at least 3.0 effective standing with the agent, their corporation or their faction, and no worse than -2.0 with anyone involved.

  • The arcs themselves run in pirate null-sec (Venal for the Guristas, Curse for the Angels), where gate camps and warp bubbles are common. A fast ship that can’t be bubbled, like an interceptor, makes life much easier.
  • They cost some empire standing: Smash and Grab lowers your Caldari standing (by 2.4% to 12%) and your Gallente standing slightly; Angel Sound lowers your Minmatar standing by about 2.4%.

Run pirate COSMOS missions One chance only

COSMOS missions are short storyline chains that give faction and corporation standing. The Guristas’ are in the E-8CSQ constellation in Vale of the Silent, and the Angels’ are in the I-3ODK constellation in Feythabolis, both in null-sec.

You can only ever do each COSMOS mission once. Failing one, or letting it expire, locks you out of that mission and the rest of its chain forever. And as soon as you talk to the agent, you get an offer that expires in seven days, so don’t speak to them until you’re ready to run it.

Boost your gains with a Glamourex booster Event or store

Glamourex boosters work like extra levels of the Social skill: the Potent version gives +50% to standing gains. Take one before an epic arc or COSMOS chain to get much more out of it. There are two ways to get them, and they behave differently:

  • From in-game events such as Capsuleer Day: these are items that stop working after a set date, so check the description before you buy one on the market.
  • In packs from the official EVE Online store: these don’t go stale. They wait in your unclaimed items until you redeem them, but they’re applied to your character the moment you redeem them and only last a limited time from then, so redeem one just before you start the arc or missions.

Use a standings service Costs ISK

Some player corporations raise NPC standings for you, for a fee paid in ISK, the in-game currency. One long-running service advertises on the official EVE forums and has a Discord server. These are run by players, not CCP, so read how they work and check their reputation before you send any ISK.
